## Why this different idea

Iteration 42's exact future one-piece line-clear accessibility (two holdouts 1967,1968) improved **all six** generator/holdout mean survival and synthetic estimated-score comparisons against unchanged v1.7, without combo or legality regressions. However shifted-heavy p10 was 0.9412x on second holdout (16 vs 17). Iteration 43b's conditional zero-hard-shape fit floor, although legal and mean-positive, lowered shifted-heavy p10 to **0.8333x** on first holdout 1971 (15 vs 18). We retained both failed metrics and did not promote. The problem appears to be structural lower-tail brittleness, not a need to endlessly retune the same four coefficients.

**New distinct targeted hypothesis:** a board may have plenty of empty cells and even some future clearing opportunities yet still have those cells scattered across separate isolated pockets too small to fit large pieces. Research-only source commit `b9bc50541e316fb24e2ddee42991ad48d55e0c77` defaults `BL_RESEARCH_HARD_ZERO_FLOOR=0`, retains `BL_RESEARCH_FUTURE_CLEAR_ACCESS=1` and adds `BL_RESEARCH_CONNECTED_FREE_REGION=1` to the late completed-three-piece afterstate reranker, not the full search. It flood-fills actual four-neighbor empty-cell connected components with exact 8x8 bitboard adjacency; when fewer than 40 cells are free, it gives a bounded penalty of 100 for each empty cell **outside the largest connected free region**, capped at 16. This distinguishes one usable contiguous region from multiple disconnected pockets; it never knows or samples hidden future RNG, and is not the failed fixed hypothetical three-piece rollout. Prior global minimax, broad all-shape mixture, baseline risk shield, dual pool, bottom-three and proactive override stay OFF by default.

Research-only selftest commit `11c83d88017b56ce613446f74c3e83d42729cc26` checks that equal-area free boards with 4 connected cells versus two disconnected 2-cell pockets receive respectively 0 and negative fragmentation penalty, as well as empty/full board cases and prior exact combo/8x8 legality tests.
